Foeman Deputy City Clerk, Billy Urquia Public Speaker(s) ______________________________________________________________________________ C-7 [Start: 9:16:39 a.m.] Resolution authorizing the appointment of City Commissioner Vince Lago to serve as an ex-officio member of the Coral Gables Museum’s Board of Directors beginning in March 2014. Mayor Cason: I would like to ask the City Attorney to explain a little bit about Item C-7, any legal aspects, this is the request to have Commissioner Lago serve as an ex-officio member of the Museum Board of Directors and I wondered if there were any restrictions in terms of voting on issues that might come before us in terms of funding or anything else for the Museum. I think it’s a great idea, but I just wanted to make clear. City Attorney Leen: Do you want me to respond before or after the vote on the Agenda? Mayor Cason: No, before. I want to vote for, it but I just wanted to… City Commission Meeting January 28, 2014 Agenda Item C-7 – Resolution authorizing appointment of Commissioner Lago to serve as ex-officio member to CG Museum Board Page 2 City Attorney Leen: My office obviously approved the resolution. I looked into this issue myself and I also called outside ethics counsel. Basically, as long as Commissioner Lago is being appointed by the Commission to fill that director position ex-officio, which makes it clear that he’s acting on behalf of the City in that position. He is the City’s representative. It’s outside ethics counsel and my own opinion that there would no conflict of interest. He is allowed to serve on that Board and on this Board and vote on matters involving the Museum on the Board, but ultimately his duty is to the City in that position. Commissioner Quesada: Let me ask you a question Craig, because I’ve sat as an ex-officio member on the B.I.D., the Chamber, and the Coral Gables Community Foundation, my understanding I don’t vote at those Board meetings because my understanding was that as an ex- officio member I shouldn’t be voting. So even though I attend the meetings I understand I contribute when they want to know what’s going on with the City. I never vote, is that the proper procedure that I’ve been handling? City Attorney Leen: It’s best to be ex-officio and to not vote just to participate in the discussion. Now outside ethics counsel when I talked to him voting itself is probably not a problem. The problem would be more with that Board. They would have to be OK with the fact that when you vote you are not voting in their – because remember when you are a director of an organization you have a fiduciary obligation to them. They need to be aware that your fiduciary obligation is to the City when you are voting and you are doing it basically as a City representative. So normally the way that that’s dealt with is you’re appointed ex-officio, you are not voting, so it’s very clear that your duty ultimately is to the City that you are acting as a City representative. Mayor Cason: But there could be a difference – you were appointed on PACE, where each City gets a vote in terms – is that a little different? City Attorney Leen: Well he’s allowed to vote on PACE for the very reason I just stated. The Commission is aware of the issue. The Commission appointed him as the representative, decisions on PACE by this Commission are legislative in nature. So he can do that, but generally the better practice is to be ex-officio because it makes it clear that you are there as the City representative, not as a director of that Board, but there is nothing impermissible about that. In fact PACE is structured in that way, every City has a representative who votes and that is fine, there is no ethical issue with that. Mayor Cason: Got you. OK. Commissioner Quesada: Then I’m sorry to continue, I’m just confused. So as an ex-officio member, should we be voting or should we not be voting at those meetings? City Commission Meeting January 28, 2014 Agenda Item C-7 – Resolution authorizing appointment of Commissioner Lago to serve as ex-officio member to CG Museum Board Page 3 City Attorney Leen: That depends on your relationship with that particular Board. You can just like with PACE, if you are appointed by – the important thing is that the City Commission has appointed you to that position, and it’s clear that you are there as an official. I talked to outside ethics counsel about this, but there is no issue. The issue is not whether you vote or not, it’s whether you are there in your official capacity. Commissioner Quesada: OK. Mayor Cason: The same for the League of Cities, we have our representative there as well. City Attorney Leen: Yes. They can vote on the League of Cities, but also they could vote on matters involving the League of Cities when they are a City Commissioner. Mayor Cason: OK. Commissioner Quesada: Understood. Thank you. [End: 9:20:12 a.m.]
